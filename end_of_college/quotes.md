# The End of College as We Know It

**Andy Kessler**, *The Wall Street Journal* (Opinion, Inside View), October 4, 2026
[Read the article](https://www.wsj.com/search?query=%22The%20End%20of%20College%20as%20We%20Know%20It%22%20Kessler) <!-- TODO: replace with article URL -->

*AI is wreaking havoc in universities, but higher ed's woes long predate ChatGPT.*

## The point

> "If universities don't change, more than a few will implode, while others will wither away over decades. AI and alternative forms of learning are already rising. Something radical is needed to stop the decay of our once great universities. Who will step up?"

## Key quotes

### 1. The cracks are already showing
> "What's the matter with universities? Syracuse missed its 2026 enrollment target and may run a $30 million deficit. Minnesota is cutting its budget by $225 million over two years. Tulsa is slashing tuition by more than half. Colleges enroll 4.2% fewer undergraduates than last year."

### 2. Reimagining isn't radical enough
> "Cornell, Yale, Vanderbilt and Washington University in St. Louis put out plans—not radical enough, if you ask me—to reimagine the university."

### 3. Don't blame AI first
> "It's easy to blame artificial intelligence, but problems run deeper"

*Context:* Introduces his list of underlying problems: aptitude, admissions, accounting, affordability, and AI.

### 4. The credential game is over
> "That led to branding and credentialism, but that game is over."

*Context:* On aptitude testing after *Griggs v. Duke Power* (1971).

### 5. Employers doubt the graduates
> "A Cornell report noted employers' "jarring" skepticism of college graduates because they lack job skills such as "the ability to handle uncertainty and solve problems that do not have clear answers.""

### 6. Even the Ivy League loses ground
> "A Forbes survey found almost half of corporate executives are either less likely to hire Ivy League graduates than they were five years ago or would never hire them."

### 7. The research model is suspect
> "That business model is now suspect."

*Context:* On universities' federal research overhead ("indirect cost rates").

### 8. Where's the value?
> "Sixteen colleges and universities cost more than $100,000 a year in tuition and expenses. Where's the value?"

### 9. Going through the motions
> "Professors and students seem to be going through the motions of learning, so there's a huge structural problem. Do ChatGPT-written essays get graded by Claude? Why bother?"

### 10. The tests failed, not the humans
> "Since then, AI has passed bar exams and medical licensing exams, which says more about these outdated tests than the inadequacy of humans, perhaps a sign of an antiquated education system."

### 11. Are teachers obsolete?
> "Even worse is a 2025 Harvard study, "AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning." Are teachers and professors becoming obsolete?"

### 12. Use AI, end the lecture
> "Universities can take advantage of AI rather than ban it. The sage-on-a-stage format is over. Learning is experiential. As corporate America rejects college branding, graduates would benefit from more hands-on experience. Universities may be uniquely positioned to implement what I call "virtual apprenticeships," real job experience while still at school."

### 13. A junior year of team projects
> "I suggest all students spend their entire junior year on team projects."

> "Cross-discipline self-organizing teams will form."

### 14. AI tutors as problems arise
> "Self-paced AI-tutored instruction can help students learn what they need as problems arise."

### 15. Public confidence has collapsed
> "According to Gallup, in 2013, 70% of Americans said college was very important. Today it's only 31%."
