## The point

> "They know its flaws, but they also know its power. And they just can't bring themselves to give that up."

## Key quotes

### 1. A fitting birthday scandal
> "The university where the term *artificial intelligence* was coined is ringing in the field's 70th birthday in fitting fashion: with an academic-integrity scandal."

### 2. Warning students with AI's help
> "Provost Santiago Schnell appeared to have relied heavily on AI to write all nine of the articles he's published this year—including a *Washington Post* op-ed about students outsourcing their writing to AI."

### 3. "Assistive tools"
> "he sent an email statement acknowledging that he uses AI chatbots as "assistive tools" in his writing but downplayed their role in his process. Even Schnell's statement, the paper found, registered as "100% AI-written" on Pangram"

### 4. Not an isolated case
> "Schnell is the latest example of an ironic phenomenon: People whose job involves writing about the appropriate uses of AI—or even setting the rules on AI use for others—keep getting caught using AI to do it."

### 5. The awkward question
> "It's another to use AI to inveigh against improper uses of AI, and it raises an awkward question: Why do some of the people warning us most loudly about the technology's pitfalls keep tumbling headfirst into them?"

### 6. Incredibly seductive
> "As a technology, it is incredibly seductive"

*Context:* Steven Rosenbaum, author of *The Future of Truth*, whose book was found to contain AI-generated misquotes.

### 7. The process is blurry
> "The line between thinking and researching and reading and outlining and drafting and writing is really blurry"

*Context:* Rosenbaum.

### 8. Readers care about the result
> "I don't think readers care how the sausage is made. They care that it's delicious."

*Context:* Rosenbaum.

### 9. Readers care who chose the words
> "I think readers value knowing that every word they're reading was chosen by the author and not an algorithm."

*Context:* Oremus, explaining his own red line: AI for questions and feedback, not for writing.

### 9a. Draw a personal red line
> "I've found that having a personal red line is both useful and clarifying: I might ask a chatbot questions, get its feedback on a word choice or an explanation, or ask it to poke holes in my argument, but I will not ask it to write for me or use its writing in my own drafts."

*Context:* Used for the deck's opening teaser slide.

### 10. Don't outsource your thinking
> "Don't cut corners. … Don't outsource your thinking, however tempting that may be."

*Context:* Cath Ellis, pro-vice-chancellor for quality and integrity at Western Sydney University, in an op-ed later found to be at least partly AI-generated.

### 11. "With" AI, not "by" AI
> "It was written with AI, and there's a really big difference there"

*Context:* Ellis, defending her op-ed.

### 12. Prose without the intellectual work
> "students can now produce proficient prose without performing much of the intellectual work the prose appears to represent."

*Context:* Schnell's op-ed. When confronted, he "claimed authorship of the "ideas and arguments" even if bots did some of the writing."

### 13. The same defense students use
> "No doubt plenty of students accused of AI cheating have mounted defenses that sounded a lot like Ellis's and Schnell's."

### 14. A rule for other people
> "*Writing is thinking* has become a common refrain among critics of those who use AI tools to do their writing. What we see again and again in defenses of AI writing is that the people using AI don't seem to apply that maxim to themselves."

> "*Writing is thinking* is a rule for other people"

*Context:* The second line is Claude's suggestion when Oremus asked it to brainstorm hypotheses.

### 15. The third-person effect
> "people's tendency to believe that they're immune to persuasive messaging even as they expect it to influence others."

### 16. Built to seem trustworthy
> "Chatbots might come with fine-print disclaimers that they can make mistakes, but they bend over backwards in their interactions with users to come across as honest and trustworthy."

### 17. We lack the words
> "society has yet to develop a vocabulary for all the ways of writing with AI that fall in between "AI-generated" and "entirely human-written.""

### 18. Even the author's line is fuzzy
> "Here's where I acknowledge that even my own bright line on AI use might be fuzzier than I realize."

> "Am *I* an AI-writing hypocrite now too?"

### 19. Risk rises with use
> "AI is a power tool, and like any power tool it's dangerous if you're careless with it. You can be the guy who warned everyone that a table saw can cut your fingers off and still lose a finger one day, because that risk is built into the machine. It goes up the more you use it."

*Context:* An anonymous journalist who once published AI-generated errors.

### 20. Too powerful to give up
> "When you think about it that way, it makes sense that some of the people relying on AI would be the same ones sounding the alarm about its risks. They know its flaws, but they also know its power. And they just can't bring themselves to give that up."
