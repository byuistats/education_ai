# Chapter 3: Hope and Love

Joseph M. Spencer, *For Zion: A Mormon Theology of Hope*, chapter 3.

## Summary

Spencer turns from Romans 4 to Romans 5, where faith, hope, and love become intertwined. Hope is not passive trust that a better future will arrive. God enlists faithful people in transforming the world, and hope becomes confidence that they can participate in creating a way where the present order provides none.

This participation strengthens hope through endurance, tested character, and sustained work. It also creates a theological danger: growing confidence can become confidence in one’s independent abilities. Paul’s turn to love answers that danger. The Spirit discloses that God’s love was already at work and transforms hope from individual instrumentality into covenantal partnership.

Love is therefore more than emotion or mutual fixation. It is an attentive way of sharing different experiences of the world while working together on something beyond the two participants. In relation to God, love becomes an unending conversation about the world’s possibilities. Hope reaches fruition as loving confidence in work shared by everyone who participates in God’s vision.

## Argument at a glance

- God transforms the world while enlisting faithful, hopeful people in that work.
- Hope joins fidelity to the past with responsibility for work in the present.
- Endurance strengthens hope, but self-confidence can obscure dependence on God.
- Love transforms hope into conversation, partnership, and shared work.
- Love directs two people toward a shared reality beyond themselves.
- Fully transformed hope is confidence in the work shared by those who participate in God’s vision.
