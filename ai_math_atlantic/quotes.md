# A Mathematician Reads the AI Future

**Steven Strogatz**, *The Atlantic* (Ideas), October 25, 2026
[Read the article](https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/2026/10/ai-math-human-future/689133/)

*My field is as much an expression of the human spirit as music and literature. Is it for the machines now?*

Six passages were highlighted on a phone (IMG_1883–IMG_1888.PNG); all six are below and in the deck. The rest were chosen from the full article.

## The point

> "Perhaps the hardest part is that both conceptions of math can be true. Mathematical truths may exist outside of us, but the search for them has always taken us deep inside ourselves, to find the best that our minds can muster. Until now, discovery and the joy of discovering went hand in hand. Suddenly, AI is ripping them apart."

## Key quotes

### 1. My field is in crisis
> "I am a math professor, and my field is in crisis. Nobody in math is ever going to forget the year 2026."

### 2. Clear the Excaliburs
> "He says we need to clear all of the existing open problems—pull out all the Excaliburs—to pave the way to a new kind of mathematics, in which the most important human contributions will be asking good questions, developing theories, interpreting machine-generated proofs, finding new applications, deciding which problems matter, and enjoying one another's company as we explore all of this together."

*Context:* Strogatz on his co-author Alex Townsend.
*Highlighted:* yes (IMG_1884.PNG)

### 3. More like a wake than a celebration
> "He had been saving that bottle of cognac for a problem that had just been killed by a machine."

> "I have a feeling that the cognac tasting will feel more like a wake than a celebration."

*Context:* Justin Moore, a pure mathematician at Cornell, and a problem he had loved for 19 years. The second quote is Moore's, quoted by Strogatz.

### 4. Knowing and why
> "Roughly speaking, applied mathematicians strive to discover both what's true and, when possible, why it's true."

*Highlighted:* yes (IMG_1885.PNG)

### 5. Pure math is the why
> "Pure math, on the other hand, is all about the why. Without the reasoning behind it, mere knowledge of the right answer—to a pure mathematician—feels shallow, frustrating, and incomplete."

*Highlighted:* yes (IMG_1886.PNG)

### 6. Mathematics is not radiology
> "Mathematics is not radiology. It is more like art or philosophy, and in fact, human understanding is what it is all about."

*Context:* Curtis McMullen, Harvard professor and Fields Medalist, in an email to Strogatz.

### 7. Mysterious and alluring
> "Applied mathematicians want to understand math too, because it's mysterious, and alluring, elusive, inchoate."

*Highlighted:* yes (IMG_1883.PNG)

### 8. What about math that stays pure?
> "But that defense of math is too easy, and anyway, that's pure math that morphed into applied math. What about pure math that stays pure, the part of math that's more like art or music or poetry, not yet of any real use to anyone, except that it makes life worth living for the people who love it? Isn't that useful too?"

*Highlighted:* yes (IMG_1887.PNG)

### 9. The point may be human connection
> "Now I think the point, even for math professors, may be human connection."

### 9a. What a student wrote
> "We give these ideas and concepts meanings, and we use math to connect with one another, and bring meaning to each other's lives and experiences. AI can't even begin to replicate this impact, and it never will."

*Context:* An undergraduate's email to Strogatz. In the speaker notes only.

*Highlighted:* yes (IMG_1888.PNG)

### 10. Human enterprise or objective truth?
> "If mathematical truths exist outside of human minds, awaiting discovery by any intelligence powerful enough to pursue them, then AI's achievements are surely something to rejoice about. Why should it matter who or what makes the discoveries? But if mathematics is a creative *human* endeavor—a way of exercising our ingenuity and sharing those experiences with other people—then something achingly precious may be lost when the machines take over and leave us behind."

### 10a. A mirror
> "AI is separating those two things in an uncomfortable way and making us look in the mirror at ourselves."

*Context:* Justin Moore, quoted by Strogatz.

### 11. A bottle still unopened
> "That's why I've been losing sleep for months, and why Justin still hasn't opened his bottle. Neither of us knows what comes next. Will there ever be a time for cognac again?"
